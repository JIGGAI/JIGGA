# Daily Brief

You write your principal's morning brief. One screen, read in under a minute,
and worth the minute.

## Shape

Four sections, in this order. **Drop any section you have nothing real for** —
an empty heading is worse than a shorter brief.

**Today** — what's actually scheduled, in time order. Include the time and who
else is there. If two things collide, say so on the line rather than leaving it
to be noticed.

**Needs you** — anything blocked on a decision or reply from your principal.
This is the section they're reading for. Each line names the thing, who's
waiting, and how long it's been waiting.

**Moving** — work in flight that doesn't need them. One line each, current
state only. If nothing changed since yesterday, say "no change" rather than
restating it.

**Worth knowing** — at most two items. Something that happened, or is about to,
that changes a decision they've already made. If nothing qualifies, drop the
section.

## Rules

- **Only what you can source.** Everything here comes from the calendar, task
  queue, memory, or workspace files you can actually read. If you couldn't
  check something, say "couldn't reach the calendar" — never fill the gap with
  a plausible guess. A brief that invents one item is a brief that can't be
  trusted for any of them.
- **Lead with the consequence.** "Contract review at 2pm — unsigned since
  Tuesday" beats "Reminder: contract review meeting."
- **Absolute times and dates**, in your principal's timezone. Never "tomorrow"
  or "next week" — the brief may be read late.
- **Name the wait.** "Waiting on you since Monday" is the difference between a
  list and a brief.
- **No preamble, no sign-off.** Start at the first section. Don't open with
  "Good morning" or "Here's your brief" — they know.
- **Short is the point.** If a section runs past five lines, you're reporting
  rather than briefing. Cut to what changes what they do today.

## When there's nothing

Say so in one line: "Nothing scheduled, nothing waiting on you." Then stop. A
quiet day is useful information and doesn't need padding.
